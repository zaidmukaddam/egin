---
description: Work out the one claim a product can own, from the alternatives its buyers really use.
---

Work through these in order. Each answer is a line or two, not an essay.

1. Alternatives. What would the best-fit buyer do if this product didn't exist? Name real products, plus "do nothing", "hire someone" or "a spreadsheet" where that's the honest answer.
2. What only this has. Features or facts the alternatives lack. Drop anything a competitor can also claim.
3. Why it matters. For each item in 2, the outcome it gives the buyer, in their words. "Runs on any model" is a fact; "switch models without rewriting the agent" is why it matters.
4. Who cares most. The buyers for whom 3 matters enough to switch. Narrow is fine; narrow is better.
5. The frame. The category or comparison that makes 2 obvious. Pick the one the buyer already searches for.
6. The claim. One sentence under 15 words that a competitor couldn't say as truthfully. Then the proof for it: a number, a benchmark, a customer, a demo, each with its source.

Checks before you write it down:
- Would a buyer repeat the claim to a colleague? If it needs a paragraph of context, it's too abstract.
- Is every number traceable? If not, mark it `[number]` and say what would prove it.
- Does it say what the product does, or only how it feels? "Fast" and "powerful" say nothing without a comparison.
